History of the Garden of Dreams

How a Rana aristocrat’s private garden became a public treasure in the middle of Kathmandu.

In the midst of downtown Kathmandu’s urban bustle, the tranquil oasis of the Kaiser Mahal Garden stands as an enduring legacy of the vision and talent of one man, Late Field Marshal Kaiser Shumsher Jung Bahadur Rana (1892–1964).

Located just across the street from the former Royal Palace at the entrance to the Thamel tourist area, the garden was neglected for decades. Its neo-classical pavilions, paying homage to Nepal’s six seasons, were crumbling, and the paths were overgrown. With the support of Austrian Development Aid and in collaboration with the Ministry of Education, Government of Nepal, this national treasure has been restored.

A statesman’s masterpiece

Kaiser Shumsher was a statesman, scholar, linguist and connoisseur of horticulture, art and literature. The garden’s design and literary allusions are inextricably linked to the books on gardening, architecture and literature in his impressive library.

Within the walls he created an ensemble of pavilions, fountains, decorative garden furniture, and European-inspired pergolas, balustrades, urns and statues. Six freestanding pavilions, each dedicated to one of Nepal’s six seasons, give the garden its architectural framework.

An Edwardian design

Paved perimeter paths, punctuated by pavilions, trellises and planting areas, surround a sunken flower garden with a large pond at its centre. In the Edwardian manner, the formal, axial architecture contrasts with the more informal, natural planting.

Kishore Narsingh, architect to Kaiser Shumsher’s father, the Maharaja, was responsible for the detailed layout. Together with his brother, he had designed and supervised the construction in 1907 of Singha Darbar for Maharaja Chandra Shumsher.
